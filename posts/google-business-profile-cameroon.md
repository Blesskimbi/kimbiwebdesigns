# Google Business Profile for Cameroonian Businesses: The Setup That Actually Ranks

Most small businesses in Cameroon that ask me about SEO want to talk about their website. Fairly often the website is not the problem. They are invisible on Google Maps, and fixing that costs nothing but an afternoon.

A Google Business Profile is the panel that appears on the right of a search result, and the three listings that show above the normal results when someone searches for a service near them. That block of three is called the map pack, and for a local service business it is usually worth more traffic than page one of the ordinary results.

I have set these up alongside client websites often enough to know where they go wrong. The mistakes are consistent, and most of them happen in the first ten minutes of setup.

## Key takeaways

- A Google Business Profile is free, and for a local business it is usually the highest return work available in local SEO.
- Distance from the searcher is a ranking factor you cannot write your way around, so the location settings matter more than the description.
- Your business name, address and phone number must be byte for byte identical on the profile, your website and every directory listing.
- Reviews are a ranking factor and a conversion factor at once. Most Cameroonian competitors have between zero and two.
- Adding keywords to your business name is against Google guidelines and is a common cause of suspension.

## Distance is a ranking factor, so get the location right first

Google decides the map pack on [three things](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091): relevance, prominence, and distance from whoever is searching. You can influence the first two with content and reviews. Distance is arithmetic.

This is why the location settings are the part to get right before you write a word of description. Two options exist and they behave differently.

If customers come to you, enter your street address and let it show. A verified address in Buea will beat a vague regional presence for anyone searching in Buea, every time.

If you go to customers, or you work from home and would rather not publish where you live, set up as a service area business. You hide the address and instead list the areas you serve. This is the right choice for most web designers, consultants, electricians and photographers.

The trap is what happens when neither is filled in properly. A profile with no address and no defined service area can end up pinned to the middle of the country, hundreds of kilometres from anyone you want to reach. Open your own listing, look at where the pin actually sits, and if it is not somewhere plausible, fix the service area before anything else.

## Your category matters more than your description

The primary category is the strongest single relevance signal on the profile. Choose the one that names what you do, not the one that sounds most impressive.

A web designer should be a "Website designer". An electrician should be an "Electrician". If you also build mobile apps, add that as a secondary category rather than reaching for something broader like "Business to business service", which tells Google almost nothing.

You can add several secondary categories. Add the ones that are true and stop there. A profile claiming nine loosely related categories looks like what it is.

## Do not put keywords in your business name

This is the most tempting mistake and the most expensive one.

Your business name field must contain your actual business name. Not "Kumba Motors, Cheapest Car Repair Douala Cameroon". [Google guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) are explicit about this, and name stuffing is one of the more commonly enforced rules. The penalty is suspension, and getting a suspended profile reinstated is considerably harder than setting one up.

There is a legitimate version of this. If your registered business name genuinely includes what you do, you are entitled to use it. The test is whether the name on your invoices matches the name on the profile.

## The name, address and phone number have to match everywhere

Google is trying to work out whether the business on the profile and the business on the website are the same entity. It does that partly by comparing the name, address and phone number across every place they appear. Marketers call this NAP consistency.

It sounds trivial and it is one of the most common quiet failures. These are all different as far as a machine is concerned:

| Where | Written as | Matches? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google profile | Bless Kimbi Web Developer | Reference |
| Website footer | Bless Kimbi | No |
| Directory listing | BlessKimbi Web Design | No |
| Invoice | Bless Kimbi Web Developer | Yes |

Pick one exact form and use it everywhere, including the structured data on your website. If your site declares a different business name than your profile, you are asking Google to connect two things that do not look connected.

While you are there, link the two directly. Put your website URL on the profile, and reference the profile from your site structured data. It removes the guesswork.

## Reviews are where this is won locally

Most web design businesses in Cameroon have between zero and two Google reviews. That is the competitive situation, and it means the bar is low enough to clear quickly.

Ten to twenty genuine reviews puts you ahead of almost everyone locally. They matter twice over: they feed prominence, which is one of the three ranking inputs, and they are the thing a person actually reads before deciding to call you.

How to get them without being irritating:

- Ask when the work is delivered and the client is pleased, not three months later.
- Send the direct review link. Asking someone to find your listing themselves loses most of them.
- Ask in the channel you already use. In Cameroon that is WhatsApp.
- Reply to every review, including the difficult ones. A calm reply to a complaint reads better to the next customer than an unbroken wall of five stars.

Never pay for reviews, never review yourself, and do not offer a discount in exchange. Google catches review manipulation and the profile is the thing you lose.

## Fill in the parts most people skip

Once the structural work is done, the rest takes an afternoon and most competitors never do it.

Add real photographs of real work, not stock images. Add your services with short descriptions and honest starting prices. Use the Q&A section by posting the questions clients genuinely ask and answering them yourself, which is allowed and is a good use of the feature. Post an update occasionally so the profile does not look abandoned.

Set your hours accurately, including the days you do not work. A profile that says you are open when you are not produces exactly one review, and it is not a good one.

## Conclusion

The order matters more than the effort. Get the location settings right, choose an accurate primary category, keep your business name honest and identical everywhere, then collect reviews steadily. Everything else on the profile is worth doing, and none of it will rescue a listing pinned to the wrong part of the country.
